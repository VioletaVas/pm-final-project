Slide 5 · Strategy

Heavy pay-later users juggle several apps. One unexplained decline sends them back to debit.

Problem hook

Riverty’s heaviest pay-later users already juggle several apps, and the test card sends them back to their debit card at the first unexplained decline, so unless one balance and reliable in-store payment ship soon, the banking licence launches a card nobody keeps in their wallet while merchants question Riverty’s checkout.

Value proposition

Pay later anywhere, with one balance and one due date for card and invoice, and clear reasons when a payment fails. For merchants, the card brings offers that send shoppers to their shops.

Why now: licence is live · heavy-user demand · Month 1 cohort activity falls 66.3% → 51.0% (month 1 to 2).

Risk verdict

Initiative-worthyNot yet a proven threat

Heavy users: 82.6% activation, €320.88/month, 25.4% instalments. Evidence base is thin: 4 months, 1,200 invitees, 12 notes, no competitor data. Launch is conditional on B1, B2, B3/B8, starting with heavy BNPL users plus a non-employee cohort.

Data-backed hypothesis

Based on N-07, tickets T-103 and T-104, and cohorts losing 11.3 to 20.5pp of active cards in the first month (51.0 to 57.1% still active in month 2), I believe that giving heavy BNPL users one balance and one due date will result in more of them still buying two months after activation, as measured by a 10pp increase in heavy-user month-2 active share. Guardrail: spend per active card ≥ €233.15. Go/no-go after two cohorts reach month 2, with 100+ heavy activated cards each.

Moment of misery

  • Systemic: in-store reliability (6.4% to 12.1% declines; 48 with no reason, T-101)
  • Systemic by design: split balances and due dates (T-103, T-104)
  • Limited reach: 143 of 431 cards never reached the wallet (T-102)
Slide 6 · Research

Workaround: six manual steps to learn one total

Competitive analysis / workaround

  1. Open each pay-later app and the Riverty card screen.
  2. Read each balance and due date separately.
  3. Sum the amounts mentally, with some external tool.
  4. Reconcile two reminders against each other (T-104).
  5. Ask support or the nearest participant which total is real (T-103).
  6. Fall back to the debit card when uncertain (N-03, N-06).

External tools: spreadsheet, notes app, banking app, calendar.

Persona

Heavy BNPL user, 18-34, runs two pay-later apps (N-01, N-02, N-04). Goal: everyday spend, including the weekly shop, without another app to monitor.

Core frustration

Not knowing what they owe in total, at the moment a payment demand arrives. T-103 and T-104 collide: two reminders, different amounts, same week, on separate screens. N-02: “one more thing to check before payday.”

Control (A)

Today’s experience: declines are silent (T-101). Balance and reminder friction (T-103, T-104) stays.

Variant (B)

One change: every decline shows a reason and one retry hint (alert, reason screen, TRY AGAIN, USE ANOTHER PAYMENT METHOD).

Held constant

App version, approval and credit policy, limits, decline rate, balance screens, wallet add, offers. Randomized per card at activation, stratified by employee vs friend/family (assumption).

Iterate +0 to 10pp

Kill no gain after two cohorts, or guardrail breached

Audience

Heavy BNPL user who runs two pay-later apps

Tier

S, Minimal

~40 declines/month (18 in-store + ~22 online, derived). No paid media, agency or video.

Channels (all owned)

Decline push: 5 s, once per event, 1-tap open Decline screen + red “Declined” row + changelog + push opt-in at activation Lifecycle email: held until readout (M tier only)

1 · Decline recovery rate (primary)

Share of declines followed by a successful card payment at the same merchant within [window]

2 · Month-2 active share

Cards with 1+ purchase in days 31 to 60 after activation; heavy users as subgroup

3 · Spend per activated card (guardrail)

Total spend ÷ all activated cards, not just active ones

Bad signals

Recovery up, month-2 flat: single payment recovered, user does not return · Recovery up, USE ANOTHER PAYMENT METHOD high: recovery via a competitor’s method · Declines per card rising: retry loops · Push disable rate up: push seen as noise.

Likely decision: Iterate (low exposure, first readout likely under-powered). Phases: Beta (baselines, dogfood) → Launch (50/50, no external comms) → Post-launch readout.

Slide 10 · Story

The decline event is the right unit of analysis

Friction points

  • Some numbers undefined.
  • Wrong baseline: 51.0% and 57.1% cover all users, my hypothesis targets heavy users. Heavy share and baseline were not in the data pack.
  • Back and forth on the feature to test: the main value feature was not a quick win, so not a NOW feature.

Aha moment

The feature has a built-in trigger, the decline event, logged in both arms. That points to decline recovery as primary metric and month-2 active share as second. It also makes the product itself the channel (the decline push), so the launch is an S-tier release, not a campaign.

Key takeaways / next

AI helped with fast artifact creation and with bouncing off ideas. Next: keep building AI literacy as a co-pilot for future product ideas and team empowerment. Pull the four baselines first to confirm the test is feasible.
